A loudspeaker converts an electronic signal into sound. A voice coil (an RL circuit) sits inside a permanent magnet; current through the coil produces a force that drives a spring-mass-damper cone. The cone's motion, in turn, induces a back-EMF in the coil, and its acceleration is what generates the sound pressure wave.

Two input voltages — a signal vs and a noise vn — are first summed through a low-pass filter (time constant τf) before reaching the coil:

Problem 4: Noise and Filter

Check "noise" to add pink noise to the input and listen again — there should now be unwanted noise. Speech and music mostly concentrate their energy below a few kHz, while broadband (pink/white) noise spans the whole audible range; a low-pass filter can only attenuate noise by also attenuating the signal wherever their spectra overlap. Choose a τf and click Update to see how the trade-off plays out.
