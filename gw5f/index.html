Learning Goals:

  • Understand how to read log-based magnitude and phase plots
  • Understand how to measure the amplitude ratio and phase difference between two sinusoids with the same frequency ω from a time history plot
  • Understand how to read an amplitude ratio and phase plot (frequency response), and generate the correct output sinusoidal signal

Consider the spring-mass-damper system mẉ+bẋ+kx=f(t) with a sinusoidal force input f(t)=F sin(ωt). The steady-state output is also sinusoidal, at the same frequency but with its own amplitude and phase:

The magnitude and phase come from the transfer function evaluated at s=jω:

The Bode phase plots below use the same continuous convention MATLAB's bode() uses: 0° at ω=0, decreasing smoothly down to −180° as ω→∞, passing through −90° at ω=ωn. Watch the sign carefully when reading values off a time-history plot instead of the Bode plot directly — it's easy to get backwards.

Problem 1: Understanding Bode Plots

Amplitude ratio and phase difference plots are typically called "Bode" plots, as Hendrik Wade Bode developed a process for hand-drawing them based on asymptotes in the 1930s.

Discuss what a Bode plot means amongst your group and write down your answer. Be thorough and make your understanding of the concept clear — be more specific than "the magnitude and phase of the frequency response."
